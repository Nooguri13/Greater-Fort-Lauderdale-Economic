01

The fifteen-year arc

Every series here is annual and inflation-adjusted or from the Census. Read together, they show an economy that grew output by half in a decade, absorbed two shocks, and shifted toward higher value per person.

Real GDP, 2010-2024

Chained 2017 $, billions - BEA
◆ Analyst's read

Output rose from $91B to $137B, a +49% real gain. The only interruption was 2020 (down 1.8%), erased within a year.

The post-pandemic run is the story: +24% from 2020 to 2024, well above the pre-COVID trend line.

Real GDP per capita, 2010-2024

Real GDP divided by population - $ per resident - BEA & Census

Output per resident climbed ~$52K to ~$68K (+31%). Because it divides GDP by population, the rise means each resident produces more, not just that there are more of them.

Percent change in real GDP

A single negative year in fifteen (2020). The +9.2% rebound in 2021 is the sharpest in the series.

Unemployment rate, 2010-2024

02

How the economy is built

A $137B economy split into its three BEA super-sectors. The shape explains the resilience: private services do the heavy lifting, and they are the fastest-growing and the largest contributor to growth.

Real GDP by super-sector, 2024

Chained 2017 $, billions - BEA
◆ Analyst's read

Private services are ~85% of output ($116B) - finance, trade, tourism, health, professional and tech services. Government is ~9%, goods-producing ~7%.

A services-weighted economy is less cyclical and more knowledge-intensive, and it is exactly where the target industries sit.

Super-sector growth, indexed 2018-2024

2018 = 100 - real GDP by segment

Services led (index 130), pulling the whole economy up while goods and government grew slower.

Share of GDP over time

Each segment as % of total, 2018-2024

The services share widened from ~82% to ~85%, a steady structural tilt, not a one-year blip.

Segment change, 2018-2024

Percent change in real GDP

Services expanded +30% in six years - roughly triple the goods sector's pace.

Who drove the growth

$B of real GDP added, 2018-2024

Of ~$29B added since 2018, services supplied $27B - the economy's single engine.

03

The labor market right now

The monthly data is subtler than the headline. Unemployment ticked up, but the job base held and gains were broad - a labor-supply normalization, not a downturn.

Unemployment rate, monthly

Mar 2025 - Jul 2026, % - not seasonally adj.

Rose from 3.3% to 4.6%, yet the labor force barely moved (down 0.1% YoY) and nonfarm jobs held near 929K - supply-side, not demand-side.

Jobs added by sector

Year over year, Aug 2025 - CareerSource Broward

Broad-based: 9 of 10 sectors gained. Higher-wage financial and professional services grew alongside tourism.

▲ The watch item

Housing affordability is the recruitment constraint

Section 01 showed incomes outrunning population, but home prices outran incomes. The median home value is roughly 5.1x median household income, well above the ~3x ratio considered affordable. That squeezes the mid-skill workforce target industries need to hire, so an honest pitch pairs recruitment with a workforce-housing strategy.
